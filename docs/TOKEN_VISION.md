# POT token concept: coordinate the network around the money

POT's core product should work with USDC. People should never need to buy a volatile asset just
to contribute to a birthday gift, create a pot, withdraw eligible funds, or claim a refund.

A token becomes useful if POT grows from one application into a network governed and improved
by its organizers, communities, and builders. The token's job would be to coordinate that shared
network, while USDC remains the money inside each pot.

This is a proposed product direction. There is no implemented POT token, issuance, or promised
token reward in this repository.

## The case for a token

An app can use ordinary loyalty points. A network used by independent communities and external
applications may need portable participation rights and transparent allocation of shared
resources. A carefully designed token could make those decisions inspectable and transferable
between participating applications, rather than keeping them in one company's private database.

That is the reason to consider a token: shared coordination and resource allocation. A token is
not needed merely because the product uses a blockchain. The design must demonstrate advantages
over non-transferable reputation and conventional governance before implementation.

## Proposed utility

### Community governance

Token holders could help allocate a defined ecosystem budget: builder grants, community pilots,
accessibility improvements, and integrations. They would not govern individual pots or gain
withdrawal rights over anyone else's funds. Existing payout and refund rules remain immutable.

Use delegated participation, published proposals, review periods, and limits on treasury powers.
Pair economic voting with non-transferable participation records so buying tokens alone does
not become the only source of influence. The exact mechanism remains an open design question.

### Sponsored participation

A capped community budget could sponsor transaction fees for eligible users. This would remove
friction from small, legitimate collections without making gas sponsorship unlimited. The
governance layer could decide the budget and eligibility; contributors would still pay in USDC
and would not need to own the token themselves.

Sponsorship must be optional and budget-controlled. A token cannot create the underlying ETH
or USDC needed to pay fees; the treasury must actually be funded.

### Useful work, recognized

Reward approved work that makes the network better: shipping an integration, improving a
translation, resolving accessibility issues, documenting a verified workflow, or operating a
community pilot. Rewards would come from a disclosed finite allocation and follow evidence-based
review. This is different from paying people for moving money through pots.

Do not reward transaction volume, circular payments, fake referrals, or the size of a pot.
Completing a pot can establish an application event, but does not prove its organizer is honest.
Fraud checks and review are needed before any public reward mechanism.

### Builder commitment, later

For future public integrations or directory listings, a refundable token bond might discourage
spam and signal commitment. This needs explicit criteria, independent review, appeals, and
precise return conditions. It must never become a vague promise that staked tokens insure a pot
or guarantee refunds. The consumer payment journey should remain available without staking.

## What the token must never do

- Replace USDC as the required contribution asset.
- Gate access to refunds, eligible withdrawals, or essential money rules.
- Give holders or administrators custody of individual pots.
- Change terms after contributors have paid.
- Promise a price, yield, profit share, guaranteed return, or guaranteed protection.
- Reward wash activity or create an incentive to collect money for its own sake.

## Credible rollout

1. Prove the full USDC journey with users and separate test wallets.
2. Measure actual repeat coordination and useful external integrations.
3. Pilot non-transferable contribution records and a transparent grants process first.
4. Publish the proposed governance, funding source, allocation, vesting, abuse controls, and
   authority boundaries. Validate whether a transferable token is necessary.
5. Obtain relevant security and legal review before implementation and distribution.

No supply, token price, sale, distribution date, or ownership promises are set here.

## Film wording

“A proposed POT token could help the community govern that network: allocate grants, sponsor
transaction fees, and reward useful contributions from organizers and builders. The principle
is simple: reward value created, not money moved. USDC remains the money in the pot. The token
supports coordination around it.”
